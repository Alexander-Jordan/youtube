### Intro

#### Short history

> In 1970, John Horton Conway, a British mathematician, designed a simulation "game" named simply "Life".
> Conway's Game of Life is not really a game in the sense that you play to win, but more about simulating cellular automation, in which cells come to life, live for some time, populate other cells, and dies.
> From 4 simple rules, fascinating and organic-like patterns emerges, and the game can keep anyone busy for some time in anticipation for what patterns can be created.

#### 20 games challenge

> I'm part of a challenge named "The 20 games challenge", in which you recreate 20 existing games.
> In this video, I'll show you how I remade Conway's Game of Life in Godot.

### Game project and basic mechanics

#### Drawing and erasing

> Like always, I start by setting up the project, and then I got into working on the base environment and modes for the game.
> Since this simulation is all about cells or tiles, I knew I wanted to explore utilizing the `TileMapLayer` node and extending it.
> And to be able to draw and erase cells using it, it was quite easy and simple.
> First I created a simple spritesheet, but really I could've just added 1 black square to it, as it was the only real required sprite for this.
> Then I made this small `Grid` script for extending the `TileMapLayer`, adding the ability to add or remove a sprite from a tile depending on the mouse position and mode.
> Then I created a small `ModeManager` to handle hotkeys and switching between the modes.
> And I even created some custom cursor icons to represent the different modes.
> And it all just worked, awesome!

#### SimulationManager

> To step into different generations, I first created a `SimulationManager`, which would both have a collection of `SimulationStep`s, and the functions to step forward, step backward, and delete a step in the simulation.
> So at first, I didn't actually implement the rules or simulated how the cells lives, populates, and dies.
> I only did the so-called wiring.
> With this implemented, it was pretty much a simple animation tool, which is not really the simulation we wanted but at least a step in the right direction, and pretty cool to be honest!

### Simulation, play modes and zoom

#### Simulation

> Now let's add the actual simulation.
> The 4 rules that make up the simulation are:
>
> 1. Any living cell with fewer than two living neighbors dies.
> 2. Any living cell with more than three living neighbors also dies.
> 3. Any living cell with two or three living neighbors lives on.
> 4. Any dead cell with exactly three living neighbors becomes alive.
>
> I updated the `Grid` script to calculate how many cells were surrounding a given cell, an based on that and the 4 rules, it calculates if the cell should be filled or empty.
> This is what is being done for all the cells in the `get_cells_after_simulation` function, and it returns a new array of all cells that is going to be part of the next simulation step.
> To generate a new generation, I just pass in the current cells into this function, and out comes the new generation of cells.
> I even made it to work with hexagon cells, and due to the different amount of possible surrounding cells compared to squared cells, the simulation plays a bit differently.
> But it works!

#### Play modes and camera

> Then I created the different simulation play modes and a way of changing the speed of the simulation.
> Now it's possible to play, stop, reset, step back or forward the simulation.
> And you can always edit the cells in any step at any time.
> Lastly for this part, I also implemented a camera zoom, so you can zoom in and out.

### Final touches

> For the finishing touches, I added all the missing UI that was left.
> So now there was this left bar for the different drawing modes, and a bottom bar for the simulation play modes.
> And the right slider is for zooming in and out, while the bottom slider is for adjusting the simulation speed.
> And with all of these in place, it's now fully playable on any mobile device as well!
> I also added an icon and a splash screen for the game, before exporting it.
