### Intro

#### Short history

> During the mid-1970s, Atari released many great games for the arcades.
> And in 1977 Atari released the home video game console that would later be known as Atari 2600, and with it came 9 games at launch.
> One of which was Indy 500, the racing game that themed around the Indianapolis 500, and was based on the earlier arcade racing game by Atari: Indy 800.

#### 20 games challenge

> I'm part of a challenge named "The 20 games challenge", in which you recreate 20 existing games.
> In this video, I'll show you how I remade Indy 500 in Godot.

### Game project & car

> This game was actually my favourite so far.
> I love racing games, but it was mainly because of 2 systems that was super fun and satisfying to create.
> The first was the car handling.
> After setting the project up, I started with a basic car script.
> I found a great guide as a starting point from the Kids Can Code website, and used it as a basis.
> Then I modified and improved it to make the car feel great to drive, and easy to improve and adjust.
> I moved some of the parts of the car script into a car properties resource, so that I could in theory switch out a cars settings with another one and it would just work.
> One part I really enjoyed was to use drag, friction, and engine power to calculate the max speed of the car.
> I used this to then apply some side slip depending on how fast you were driving, which made the car feel a lot more "real".
> This was a rabbit hole I loved exploring, and I learned a thing or two about physics and math formulas.
> So now I had a car that I was super proud of!

### Multiplayer & timer

> The second system that was fun to make and that I ended up super proud of was the `Tracker` - the timer, lap, sector, and checkpoint system.
> The easy part was to add the second car to drive around with, but then it took some time and iteration to get the `Tracker` to work with both cars in 2 modes: time trial and race.
> I had the F1 games as an inspiration for the UI, how the time trial worked, and the use of sectors, and I had plenty of fun getting it **just** right!
> The `Tracker` class is responsible to keep track of where the assigned car is positioned in a given mode.
> It uses checkpoints to define sectors, sectors to define a lap, and laps to determine the process and position of a car during a race or time trial.
> At the end of it all, I'm very pleased and satisfied with the result!

### Track

> The track was planned to be simple, since it should all fit in one static screen.
> So I created the sprites for the asphalt, grass, dirt, and some racing curb.
> With it, I built out a decent and curvy track to make it as interesting I could with the limitations I had.
> To penalize the player for going out of the track, I made a `SlowerArea` class which extends an `Area2D` and slows down the car by increasing the cars friction while inside this area.
> I also modified the UI a bit during this step, and added a countdown timer before starting a race.
> Now we are getting somewhere!

### Final touches

> Now when the game was pretty much done, I only had some finishing touches left.
> One of which was audio.
> I added audio for the `Tracker` system, so for the countdown timer and starting, when passing a checkpoint or finishing a sector, and when finishing a lap or session.
> I then added some simple engine sound for the car, and of course added some energetic music.
> At this point I also implemented a saving system, so that your track times were saved, and the best lap could forever be something you'd wanna brag about.
> Lastly, I created and added an icon and a splash screen.

### Rebranding

> Some time after finishing the game, I returned back to it to make it more copyright friendly.
> Since I've already made everything myself and made it quite unique, I only had to change the name of the game, and modify the splash screen to have the new name: Rally Racer.

### Outro
