### Intro

#### Short history

> Back in 1962, a few students at MIT released Spacewar! to showcase what the new PDP-1 minicomputer was able to do.
> Spacewar! ended up not only being the first game to be installed and played on multiple computers, but also one of the most important and influential games in the early history of video games.
> It was included in a list of the top 10 most important video games in history, got in to the World Video Game Hall of Fame in 2018, and inspired games like Galaxy Game, Computer Space, and Asteroids.

#### 20 games challenge

> I'm part of a challenge named "The 20 games challenge", in which you recreate 20 existing games.
> In this video, I'll show you how I remade Spacewar! in Godot.

### Game project and ships

> In the first step I setup the the game project and the players ships.
> I used Aseprite to make a simple ship, and added it to the ship scene.
> Then I implemented the movement, with one function for rotating, and one for accelerating.
> After adding a second ship and controls for it, I had 2 ships that I could fly around with!

### Shooting & collisions

> From earlier games in this series I had some components that I could reuse for spawning, despawning, and torpedos.
> Each ship have a `Destructable` component, which despawns the ship when destroyed.
> So to the new torpedo I added a `Destructer` component, which destruct any `Destructable` upon colliding with it.
> I also used my `Spawner` and `Spawnable` components to spawn the torpedos from the player ships.

### Screen wrapping & star

> In the original game you also had to avoid the big star or planet in the middle, which continuously pulled you in, and destroyed the ship on impact.
> So that's what I implemented, reusing the `Destructor` component on the planet scene.
> At this stage I also implemented the simplest screen wrapping imaginable, using the built-in Godot function `wrapf()`.
> All it wants is the value to wrap (which is the x or y position), and the min and max value (which is the window boundaries).
> And just like that it works perfectly, both with ships and torpedos!

### Final touches

> With the basics done, it's time for small adjustments.
> First I made the ships respawn a few seconds after being destroyed.
> Then I added particles for the thrust and explosions, matching the color of the ship.
> Sounds were missing, so I added some SFX and music that helped with the mood.
> Then I made the UI, and also added some gameplay.
> Each player had 3 lives, and the player that lives the longest wins.
> And then the last step for the game was to add a game icon and splash screen.

### Outro

> And that's it!
> A fun little game that didn't take much effort, so I would even recommend it as your first game to make.
> The game is playable on Itch, and the source code can be found on GitHub.
> Both are linked in the description.
> Thanks for watching and I hope to see you in the next one where I remake Indy 500.
> Bye!
